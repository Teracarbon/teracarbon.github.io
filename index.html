Company Mission

A ground-up redesign of DAC.

We seek a practical route towards remediating teratons of Terran atmospheric carbon that will require terawatts of clean power. Achieving this at target costs of $100 USD ton-1 CO2, would facilitate access to a global market for direct air CO2 capture (DAC) valued in teradollars. However, tera-scale deployment of existing DAC technologies would consume a significant fraction of current and projected clean energy capacity on Earth. This reduces the entire space of possible DAC solutions to two sets: (1) technologies that increase the supply and drive down the cost of energy needed to for run exisiting, power-intensive capture applications or (2) novel technologies that capture atmospheric CO2 with minimal energy demand. We embody the latter approach, pursuing a demand-side technology that actually stores energy concurrently with CO2 removal.

Our Plan Involves:

Mitigating energetic losses via recovery

Swing-cycle capture methods theoretically allow for energy recovery. We achieve this using room-temperature electrochemistry rather than inefficient heat cycles.

Improving process kinetics

Biochemical catalysis is used to remove a key bottleneck in CO2 removals, speeding up capture.

Resolving MRV issues through triply-redundant quantification

By virtue of the system design, we can measure captured CO2 quantitatively by at least three distinct chemical methods. This resolves the measurement, reporting and verification (MRV) issues of many carbon removal methods.

Reallocating electricity, CO2 storage/resale & quantitative CO2 credits

Our process design opens up multiple business models, easing the path towards system scalability and financial sustainability.

Technology

A hybrid process for low-temperature regeneration and energy recovery.

Overview

Our flagship technology combines hydration catalysis with a supercapacitor, to drive rapid uptake of CO2 in air. During this process electrical energy required for the process of capture is stored and later recovered when CO2 is collected.
